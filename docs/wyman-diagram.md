# Wyman Diagram — Conceptual Spec

A visual way to "read" a basketball roster like a puzzle.

## 1. The Core Idea: A Roster as a Puzzle

Think of a team's roster as a jigsaw puzzle. The goal isn't just to have a lot of pieces (players), but to have pieces that fit together perfectly.

- **The Box:** The entire puzzle board represents your total roster "budget" and potential.
- **The Shapes:** Each player is a piece with a specific shape. The best pieces are "squares" or "hexagons" because they fit flush against others, leaving little empty space. Pieces like "pentagons" or "circles" have angles or curves that create gaps—meaning they're harder to build a winning team around.

## 2. What the Shapes Mean (Conceptually)

Each shape tells you something about a player's role and fit.

- **Square (Ideal):** A reliable, all-around player. Think of a 3-and-D guy who doesn't have glaring weaknesses on either end. He's the most "plug-and-play" piece.
- **Hexagon (Ideal):** A playmaker. Someone whose strength is passing and connecting with others. They make the pieces around them better.
- **Pentagon (Watch Out):** A star on offense but may struggle on defense. Great in theory, but hard to pair with other stars without creating a weak spot.
- **Circle (Watch Out):** A strong scorer but a defensive liability. A roster full of these can be unstable, like a house of cards.

## 3. Example Rosters: Efficiency in Action

### The Champion Puzzle (e.g., 2017 Golden State Warriors)

- **What it looks like:** Mostly squares and hexagons packed tightly together with very little empty space.
- **Why it works:** Players like Stephen Curry and Kevin Durant were nearly perfect building blocks. Their shapes fit together seamlessly, creating a highly efficient and adaptable team.

### The "Mixed Fit" Puzzle (e.g., Current Dallas Mavericks)

- **What it looks like:** A mix of shapes with visible gaps (empty space).
- **The story:** You have a great piece in the middle (Cooper Flagg, a hexagon), but surrounding him are pieces like Kyrie Irving (pentagon) and P.J. Washington (circle). The angles don't align perfectly, meaning the team might struggle to mesh different playstyles.

### The "All-Star Chaos" Puzzle (Hypothetical)

- **What it looks like:** A board covered in big, flashy pieces (circles and pentagons) but with huge empty spaces between them.
- **The problem:** It looks impressive on paper, but there's no "glue" connecting the stars. Defenses can exploit the gaps, and the team lacks balance.

## 4. The Big Picture: Efficiency & Connections

The best rosters are like well-oiled machines. They maximize "square" and "hexagon" pieces because they create strong, interconnected units. Rosters with too many "pentagons" or "circles" might have star power but suffer from structural inefficiencies and poor connections.

## 5. Simple Rules for Building a Strong Diagram

1. **Prioritize Squares:** Aim for reliable, two-way players to fill the center.
2. **Surround with Hexagons:** Place playmakers next to your squares to maximize passing.
3. **Minimize Gaps:** Avoid putting too many "pentagons" or "circles" together, as they create empty space and compatibility issues.

## In this app (D-50)

Each decision page centers on one Wyman-style board, drawn from 2026 Red Sox data rather than the basketball examples above:

- **The box** is the most this roster pool could field in hindsight: the tightest fit, with platoons and position moves, packed until it reaches the lid.
- **The pieces** are the scenario's nine lineup slots, sized by actual 2026 runs and dropped in by gravity. Left and right halves are the player's split against left- and right-handed pitching.
- **Empty space** is literal: gaps where outlines don't nest, and headroom between the top of the pile and the lid. Headroom is value the lineup leaves off.
- **Off the field** is an open tray beside the board with everyone the lineup leaves out, at the same scale.

The shapes follow the repository's rubric v2 ([Shape taxonomy](SHAPE_TAXONOMY.md)), not the four roles above. In particular, a Star is a player with quirks who is hard to fit, not a star player. The fitted case, the engine's pool fit, the interaction map, and the slot bars are subpages behind the board.
